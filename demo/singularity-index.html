Modified Multiscale Singularity Index

Fully automatic detection of channels across a wide range of widths, returning centerline, orientation and width from a single contrast image (Isikdogan, Bovik & Passalacqua, 2015).

Curvilinear detectionCenterlineWidthMultiscale

What it computes

The multiscale singularity index (Muralidhar, Bovik & Markey, 2013) responds strongly to curvilinear structures and weakly to step edges — so unlike a simple water threshold it detects channels rather than just land–water boundaries. Isikdogan et al. (2015) modified it to cope with the very wide span of channel widths found in deltas, producing channel centerline, orientation and width from a single-band contrast image with no manual masking.

The input is any image where water and non-water contrast, such as a NIR band or a water index; the original authors used MNDWI.

The singularity index

At each pixel the algorithm first estimates the direction θ orthogonal to the local curvilinear feature, then evaluates the index at scale σ from zero-, first- and second-order Gaussian derivatives along that direction:

Index (Isikdogan et al. 2015, eq. 1) ψf(x,y,σ) = |f0,θ,σ · f2,θ,σ| / (1 + |f1,θ,aσ|)
a = 1.7754 maximises sidelobe attenuation; window size = 6σ.

It is evaluated over N logarithmically spaced scales σn = σ1·√2 (n−1), and the per-pixel response is the maximum across scales. The index keeps its polarity: channels and islands respond with opposite signs, so discarding the negative polarity removes the island response.

The two modifications

Per-scale debiasing. Instead of subtracting one large Gaussian (which loses fine detail across a wide scale range), the input is debiased at every scale by local mean removal:

Debiasing (eq. 3) Iσ = I − Gσ ∗ I

Adaptive smoothing. Taking the maximum over many scales creates ripples near the banks of wide rivers; a scale-dependent box filter (computed efficiently with an integral image) smooths coarse scales more than fine ones to remove them.

Width & centerline

Channel width is estimated by interpolating around the best-responding scale m = argmaxn ψ:

Width (eq. 4) w(x,y) = k · Σi=−1..1 σm+i·ψ(σm+i) / Σi=−1..1 ψ(σm+i)

For centerlines, the orientation at the maximum-response scale gives the dominant direction; non-maxima suppression is applied along it, an Otsu threshold T is found, and a hysteresis rule (error threshold ε = 0.1·T) plus connected-component pruning preserves channel connectivity. Drawing a segment of length w and orientation θ at each centerline pixel regrows the channel map.

Assumptions & limitations

  • A channel wider than the largest scale (≈ image dimensions) is not detected; conversely the map also captures ponds and small water bodies up to that scale.
  • Width is measured in pixels × resolution, so 30 m Landsat sets the floor — sub-pixel channels are missed.
  • The authors report 91–98% accuracy on three test deltas against reference networks, but results depend on the quality of the input contrast image.
