Spectral water indices

Normalized-difference ratios that separate open water from land in optical imagery, producing the binary water mask that later steps build on.

MNDWINDWILandsat opticalThresholding

What it computes

Water absorbs strongly in the near-infrared (NIR) and shortwave-infrared (SWIR) while reflecting comparatively more in the green band. Normalized-difference indices exploit that contrast to give each pixel a value in [−1, 1], high for water and low for land.

NDWI (McFeeters, 1996) uses Green and NIR. It enhances open water but tends to read built-up surfaces as water. MNDWI (Xu, 2006) replaces NIR with SWIR, which suppresses built-up land, soil and sparse vegetation far more effectively — so it is DeltaSAT's default for delta water mapping.

Indices NDWI = (Green − NIR) / (Green + NIR)
MNDWI = (Green − SWIR) / (Green + SWIR)

Band mapping (Landsat 8 / 9 OLI)

GreenBand 3 (≈ 0.53–0.59 µm)
NIRBand 5 (≈ 0.85–0.88 µm)
SWIR-1Band 6 (≈ 1.57–1.65 µm)

Band numbers differ for Landsat 5/7 (TM/ETM+) and for Sentinel-2; the toolkit maps them automatically per sensor.

Thresholding to a water mask

A pixel is classified as water where the index exceeds a threshold T. A fixed T = 0 is a common starting point, but DeltaSAT also offers scene-adaptive Otsu thresholding, which selects T from the image histogram.

Classification water(x) = 1 if index(x) > T, else 0

On Sentinel-2. Sentinel-2 MSI is optical, so MNDWI and NDWI apply directly: use Green (B3) with SWIR (B11) for MNDWI and Green (B3) with NIR (B8) for NDWI. Its 10–20 m bands resolve narrower channels than Landsat at 30 m; the two optical archives can be harmonised into one denser time series.

Assumptions & limitations

  • Turbid, sediment-laden or very shallow delta water shifts index values toward land, so a single global threshold under-detects it.
  • Thin cloud, cloud shadow and terrain shadow create false-positive water.
  • At 30 m resolution, mixed pixels blur narrow channels and the exact shoreline.
  • The optimal threshold is scene- and season-dependent and should be validated, not assumed.

References

  2. Xu, H. (2006). Modification of NDWI to enhance open water features in remotely sensed imagery. Int. J. Remote Sensing, 27(14), 3025–3033.
  3. Otsu, N. (1979). A threshold selection method from gray-level histograms. IEEE Trans. SMC, 9(1), 62–66.
