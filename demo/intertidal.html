Intertidal detection & water occurrence

Mapping the zone that is alternately submerged and exposed across a year, by combining per-pixel water frequency with the JRC Global Surface Water dataset.

Water occurrenceJRC GSW fusionYearly tidal flats

What it computes

Across a year, satellite observations capture the surface at different tide stages. For each pixel, water occurrence is the fraction of valid (cloud-free) observations classified as water. Permanent water approaches 1, permanent land approaches 0, and intertidal flats fall in between because they are wet at high tide and dry at low tide.

Per-pixel occurrence occurrence(x) = Nwet(x) / Nvalid(x)
intertidal where  Tlow < occurrence < Thigh

Fusion with JRC Global Surface Water

The JRC Global Surface Water dataset (Pekel et al., 2016) provides a validated per-pixel occurrence built from the full Landsat archive (1984–present). DeltaSAT combines the scene-derived yearly occurrence with the JRC layer to (a) reliably mask permanent water and (b) stabilise classification in years with few clear observations.

Intermittent water is not necessarily tidal. Seasonal river flooding, rainfall ponding and reservoir drawdown also produce intermediate occurrence. The occurrence method gives a robust first-order delineation; resolving tides specifically requires tide-stage information.

Assumptions & limitations

  • Observation bias. Satellites overpass at a fixed local time and clouds remove scenes, so the captured tidal range is incomplete — tidal flats can be under- or over-mapped.
  • Not a tide-resolved surface. More rigorous mapping models the waterline against tide height/elevation (Murray et al., 2019; Sagar et al., 2017). Occurrence is a frequency proxy, not an intertidal DEM.
  • Classification error in the underlying water masks propagates directly into occurrence.

References

  2. Murray, N.J. et al. (2019). The global distribution and trajectory of tidal flats. Nature, 565, 222–225.
  3. Sagar, S. et al. (2017). Extracting the intertidal extent and topography of the Australian coastline. Remote Sensing of Environment, 195, 153–169.
