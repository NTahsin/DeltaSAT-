Centerline, channel & bank migration

Measuring the lateral movement of channels and their banks between two or more epochs.

CenterlineChannel migrationBank migration

Centerline extraction

The channel centerline is the reference line for migration. DeltaSAT obtains it either from the Modified Multiscale Singularity Index (orientation plus non-maxima suppression) or by computing the medial axis (skeleton) of the binary water mask.

Channel migration rate

Two centerlines from epochs t1 and t2 are compared. Two standard estimators:

Transect method Mc = ( Σk dk / n ) / Δt
dk = perpendicular distance between centerlines at transect k
Area method Mc = Aswept / ( L · Δt )
Aswept = area between the two centerlines, L = reach length

Bank migration rate

The same transect logic is applied to the bankline (the water–land boundary) rather than the centerline. Movements are separated into erosion (bank retreat) and accretion (bank advance), typically reported per bank (left/right) and as a net value, in metres per year.

Mind the detection limit. The smallest reliable migration equals roughly the co-registration error plus the pixel size. With 30 m Landsat, sub-30 m movement sits within noise — migration must exceed registration uncertainty to be meaningful, and that uncertainty should be reported alongside every rate.

Assumptions & limitations

  • Transect spacing and centerline smoothing materially change the result; document both.
  • Comparing a high-flow and a low-flow image conflates true migration with stage-driven width change. Use comparable flow conditions, or prefer the centerline, which is less stage-sensitive than the bankline.
  • Cutoffs and avulsions break one-to-one transect matching and must be handled reach by reach.
