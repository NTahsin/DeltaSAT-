Connected-component river extraction

Isolating the channel network from a binary water mask by grouping touching water pixels and discarding spurious patches.

Connected componentsMorphologyArea filter

What it computes

A raw water mask contains more than the river: ponds, lakes, flooded fields, aquaculture and isolated noise pixels. Connected-component labelling partitions the mask into discrete objects of mutually touching water pixels; the toolkit then keeps the objects that make up the channel network and removes the rest.

Definition pixels p, q are connected if a path of water pixels links them under the chosen neighbourhood (N4 or N8).
keep component C if area(C) ≥ Amin  and/or  C intersects a river seed.

Procedure in DeltaSAT

  1. Start from the binary water mask (spectral index or SAR backscatter).
  2. Optional morphological opening/closing to remove speckle and bridge single-pixel gaps.
  3. Label connected components under 8-connectivity.
  4. Retain components by area threshold Amin and/or by intersection with a user seed or the largest water body; drop the others.

4- vs 8-connectivity. Under 8-connectivity, diagonally touching pixels join the same object; under 4-connectivity they do not. The choice affects whether thin, near-diagonal channels stay connected.

Assumptions & limitations

  • Cloud or shadow gaps can split one real channel into several components.
  • Amin trades noise removal against the deletion of small but real tributaries.
  • Distributaries that disconnect at low flow may be dropped entirely.
  • Connectivity has no concept of "river" — it will keep any large blob, including a lake. For curvilinear discrimination, pair this step with the Modified Multiscale Singularity Index.

References

  1. Pavelsky, T. & Smith, L. (2008). RivWidth: a software tool for the calculation of river widths from remotely sensed imagery. IEEE GRSL, 5(1), 70–73. (Motivates a clean, connected water mask.)
  2. Standard connected-component labelling and mathematical morphology (e.g., Gonzalez & Woods, Digital Image Processing).
